A few months ago, I [started studying philosophy](https://patrickdesjardins.com/philosophy/philosophy-journey) and within a few weeks I was taking page after page of notes using [Trilium](blog/trilium-note-taking-with-mini-pc-server-host). I initially liked it, but I missed taking notes with a pen. Something about scribbling, annotating, and illustrating my thoughts helps me focus and retain better than copy-pasting or typing. When I was doing my [master's degree](blog/i-completed-my-master-degree), I opted for OneNote on my touch PC with a pen. However, that laptop is now old, the battery lasts three hours at most, it is heavy to carry, and reading on a PC is not great for long sessions. I started with paper, but printing hundreds of PDF files was not something I wanted to repeat for every course.

Epaper tablets are getting better and have a few advantages: long battery life (over 12 hours) and screens that work well in many lighting situations, like at the beach or in my bed. Also, writing feels more like paper than a screen. Compared to an iPad, I also appreciate the simplicity and the lack of distraction.

![](/images/blog/epaper_boox1.jpg)

Finding the right device was interesting as the market is expanding. I avoided any Amazon product because I do not want to be locked into the Amazon ecosystem, and I finally opted for a [Boox 10.3](https://shop.boox.com/products/go103). I wanted a device under $400, with a pen, and optionally a backlight I could turn on. Another requirement was that my PDF annotations must synchronize back to my PC through a cloud provider. Boox supports Google Drive, which was perfect for me. I do not use it to buy or borrow books, but it would also work with my library since it is, after all, an Android 15 device.

![](/images/blog/epaper_boox2.jpg)

Reading is great on the 10-inch screen. The tablet is lightweight, so reading sessions work well even when I am not at a table. The size lets me split the screen between a PDF and a note document, which I do sometimes when reviewing. Otherwise, I like writing in the margins. For web readings that are not PDFs, I copy the content into a Google Doc with a two-inch right margin, giving me even more space for notes.

![](/images/blog/epaper_boox3.jpg)

There are still some flaws with the device I am using. For example, moving from one file to another is slow. Epaper rendering is far from what a computer offers. I do not notice it when reading, writing, or even scrolling, but jumping back and forth between files is slow. The battery is nice, but I was expecting more. It is not like my e-ink watch that lasts a month. That said, given how thin the device is, and since I never study for more than 12 hours at a time, it is reasonable. Charging from 20% to 80% takes about two hours, and you can use the device while it charges. The pen is also chargeable with the same USB-C cable.

I was hesitant to purchase yet another device. It seems we already have enough with a mobile, a PC, a laptop, and a work machine. But I am glad I did. I carry it everywhere because of how convenient it is and how long the battery lasts, especially when not actively used. I will soon take a trip to Canada, and I feel it will be the perfect companion in my backpack.
